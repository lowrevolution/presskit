What makes it different

You build the gun

A weapon is a powering, a weapon and a rider. The powering decides when it may fire and what that costs — an energy pool, a heat sink that locks you out if you push it, a flywheel you wind up by standing still, a tithe paid in your own health, or a gun that only fires on the beat of the music. Over three hundred authored combinations, and each powering is at budget when it is up: they differ in how they fail, never in how much they pay.

There are no audio files

Ten music tracks and ninety-nine sound effects are synthesized sample by sample on an emulated eight-voice sound chip. The score reads the run — it sinks into its own low end when you are losing, speeds up when you take a power-up, and audibly thins out when a firefight steals its voices, because on real hardware it would have.

Nothing here is a file

The game ships with zero binary assets. Every sound, shader, sprite, particle, the store art and the executable icon are drawn by the game's own code. The whole thing is Lua and the archive contains nothing but source.

Features

  • Three modes — Quick Play (a single descent), Endless (climb until it kills you), and the Gauntlet (a run of levels with a boss at the end).
  • Five bands of hell — MORTAL is the game. Clear it and HELL I opens, then II, III, IV. Each band buys new threats rather than longer health bars: mobs start carrying your own weapons against you, minibosses appear inside ordinary waves, and bosses arrive as formations of linked bodies.
  • The Audio Studio — an unlockable music tool inside the game. Pick a track, drive the tempo, stack a second layer in sync, drag a ten-band EQ, watch a spectrum analyser, and record the result to a .wav you can open anywhere.
  • 223 achievements paying out score, mode unlocks and 23 cosmetics — trails, a hue-rotated arsenal, costumes, and nine followers including three pocket bosses.
  • Seven enemy affixes and themed packs, each taught once and then remembered forever.
  • Hunts — a mimic disguised as a wall, a quarry that runs the fight toward you, an assassination target behind a ring of guards.
  • Deep accessibility options — bloom, dynamic lighting, wall shadows, screen shake and hit stop each with their own switch and strength, plus auto-aim and full rebinding.

Long — the store description, verbatim

HELL IS NEON COLORED

Descend through a procedurally built underworld drawn in five colours and lit entirely by the things trying to kill you. Every floor is generated the moment you enter it. Every weapon is something you assembled. Every sound you hear — the score, the explosions, the sword — is synthesized note by note while you play. There are no audio files in this game.

BUILD THE WEAPON, NOT THE LOADOUT

A weapon here is three decisions.

A powering decides when it may fire and what that costs — an energy pool, a heat sink that locks out when you push it, a magazine, a flywheel you wind up by standing still, a tithe paid in your own health, or a gun that only fires on the beat of the music.

A weapon decides what comes out.

A rider decides what happens next.

Over three hundred authored combinations, and every pairing is balanced by construction: the powerings differ in how they fail, never in how much they pay. Play the one that matches how you move.

FIVE BANDS OF HELL

MORTAL is the base game. Clear it and descend to the HELL difficulties. Difficulty here buys new threats rather than longer health bars: mobs start carrying your own weapons against you, minibosses appear inside ordinary waves, and bosses arrive as formations of linked bodies — a bar that sweeps, a serpent that follows its own head, a pair bound together until the last phase splits them.

A SOUND CHIP, NOT A SOUNDTRACK

Ten chiptune tracks and ninety-nine sound effects run on one emulated eight-voice sound chip, generated sample by sample as you play. The score reads the run: it speeds up when you grab a power-up, sinks into its own low end when you are losing, and audibly thins out when a firefight steals its voices — because on real hardware, it would have.

Unlock the Audio Studio and it is handed to you. Pick the track, drive the tempo, stack a second layer in sync with the first, drag a ten-band EQ, watch a spectrum analyser, and record the result to a .wav file you can open anywhere.

Audio

The soundtrack is not a set of files. It is a synthesizer and a sequencer that run while you play, on a deliberately constrained emulated sound chip: 32 kHz, sixteen-bit stereo, exactly eight voices, one global echo. Ten tracks and ninety-nine sound effects share those eight voices, which is why a dense firefight audibly thins the music out — the effects take the melody's voices, exactly as they would have on the hardware this is modelled on.

Every track's loudness was measured rather than chosen: all ten sit within 0.05 LU of each other under ITU-R BS.1770 K-weighting, so switching track is never a jump in volume. What is deliberately not flattened is each track's own rise from calm to panic — the score getting louder as the player gets hurt is the feature.

The Audio Studio is a streaming feature. It hands the whole score to the player: pick a track, drive the tempo, stack a second layer locked to the first's step clock, drag a ten-band EQ, watch a spectrum analyser, and record to a .wav file that opens in any editor. Creators can make their own channel music out of it on stream and take it away with them.

About the developer

Low Revolution is Aaron, who goes by Revoincubus — a solo developer working in Ireland.

“I'm a full-time cybersecurity consultant, and Hell is Neon Colored is one of my passion projects. My approach to design comes out of Hemingway's approach to writing: cut everything to its core elements, and leave nothing in that isn't doing work. I'm pretty obsessed with doing that without ever sacrificing fun or visual joy — a game cut to the bone should still be a delight to look at and instantly readable at a glance. That is why this one is five colours, no art assets at all, and a soundtrack that writes itself while you play.”

Technical notes

Hell is Neon Colored is built on LÖVE 11.5 — the same Lua framework behind Balatro — and ships with no binary runtime assets whatsoever. The distributable archive contains Lua source and three text files. Everything else, from the bloom and the dynamic lighting to the sound chip, the ninety-nine effects and the store artwork on this page, is generated by that code at runtime or at build time.
